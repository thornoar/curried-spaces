Traditionally, multivariable functions in analysis are presented as f : \R ^n -> \R. At the same time, there is an idea from lambda calculus called "currying", which allows to "partially apply" a multivariable function, resulting in a function of *one* real variable, that returns another function. I have built some preliminary theory for studying curried multivariable functions, and now I wonder how this theory may be applied. In particular, I h ave defined the space C_n (\R) of continuous curried functions, and the spaces D_n^m (\R) of continuously differentiable curried functions. I have shown that C_n (\R) naturally corresponds to the space of continuous functionns from \R^n to \R, and that D_n^m (RR) corresponds to C^m (\R^n). I have also shown that C_n (\R) and D_n^m (\R) are Frechet spaces. I hope to be able to derive novel results in multivariable calculus by applying the tools of functional analysis to the spaces I described above. In particular, the motivation is "treating a multivariable function as a single-variable function", so it is natural to look for some statements that are true in the single-variable case but not true in the multivariable case. Could you give me some thoughts?
